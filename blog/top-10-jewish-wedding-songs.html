Top 10 Jewish Wedding Songs for Every Part of Your Simcha

From a tender walk to the chuppah to the moment the chairs rise above the hora, these songs help every part of the day feel unmistakably Jewish.

A great Jewish wedding playlist does more than fill the dance floor. It carries the couple and their guests through distinct moments: anticipation before the chuppah, blessings and celebration after the ceremony, the first rush of the hora, and the late-night dance set.

The best list is rarely all traditional or all modern. It mixes familiar melodies that bring generations together with songs that sound like the couple. Use these ten Jewish wedding songs as a starting point, then choose the versions, arrangements, and order that fit your community and your simcha.

1. Od Yishama

Best for: Ceremony recessional, reception entrance, or the first Jewish dance set

“Od Yishama” is built around the joyful image of voices of gladness, including the voices of a groom and bride. That wedding connection makes it a natural choice when the ceremony turns into celebration. A stately arrangement can accompany the couple out from the chuppah, while a faster version can open the dancing.

Because there are many recordings and melodies, send the exact version you love to your band or DJ. The title alone may not identify the arrangement you have in mind.

2. Siman Tov u’Mazel Tov

Best for: Breaking the glass, recessional, or a family singalong

Few songs communicate “Mazel Tov” as quickly as this one. Its repeated blessing for good fortune gives everyone an easy way to join the celebration, including guests who do not know much Hebrew.

Try a short, bright version immediately after the glass breaks or save a full-band arrangement for the couple’s reception entrance. It also works well as a bridge between formal traditions and the high-energy party.

3. Hava Nagila

Best for: The hora and chair lifting

“Hava Nagila” is the song many guests expect to hear at a Jewish wedding, and its joyful, accelerating energy makes it a dependable hora choice. It is especially useful at an interfaith or multicultural wedding because people often recognize the melody even if this is their first time joining the circle.

Ask the band or DJ to build the tempo gradually. The couple and their families need a moment to find the circle before the chairs come out, and the strongest hora feels exciting without becoming chaotic.

4. Dodi Li

Best for: Processional, chuppah entrance, or first dance

Drawn from the Song of Songs, “Dodi Li” centers on the bond between beloveds. That intimate meaning makes it one of the most flexible Jewish wedding songs: it can feel prayerful under the chuppah, romantic during a first dance, or quietly elegant during dinner.

Choose the arrangement carefully. A sparse acoustic or vocal version creates a tender ceremony moment, while a fuller contemporary recording can support a slow dance without losing the song’s Jewish character.

5. Erev Shel Shoshanim

Best for: Processional, cocktail hour, or dinner

With its graceful melody and imagery of an evening of roses, “Erev Shel Shoshanim” brings softness to a day filled with big emotions. It works beautifully as an instrumental processional or as warm background music while guests gather.

This is a good choice for couples who want Israeli music woven into the wedding without asking every song to carry the dance floor.

6. Mi Adir

Best for: Groom’s entrance or ceremony under the chuppah

“Mi Adir” is closely associated with welcoming and blessing the groom. Its ceremonial character gives the entrance weight without making the moment feel heavy. Depending on your tradition, a cantor, vocalist, or band may perform it as the groom approaches the chuppah or once the ceremony begins.

Coordinate this selection with your rabbi, cantor, and musicians so the version and placement match your family’s customs.

7. Im Eshkachech

Best for: A reflective chuppah moment or before breaking the glass

“Im Eshkachech” draws from the words “If I forget you, Jerusalem.” At a wedding, it can create a still, meaningful pause amid the joy and connect the celebration to Jewish memory and Jerusalem.

This is usually not a dance-floor song. Let it hold a specific emotional moment, and avoid placing it where guests expect the energy to rise. Your officiant can help decide whether it belongs in your ceremony.

8. Bashert

Best for: First dance, montage, or dinner

Start with “Bashert” by The Middle East Peace Orchestra, included in this Jewish wedding playlist. Different artists use the same title, so send your DJ the exact recording. Listen through it together before choosing a section for your dance, montage, or dinner.

It is also a lovely storytelling idea for couples whose relationship felt improbable, long-awaited, or meant to be.

9. Hinei Ba HaShalom

Best for: Reception entrance or upbeat dance set

Try Dovid Lowy's “Hinei Ba Hashalom” in the wedding playlist for a short, upbeat reception cue. Listen to the exact version with your DJ and decide where it should lead next, rather than relying on the title alone.

Pair it with a few familiar classics. Newer tracks land best when guests already feel confident joining the celebration.

10. Mahapecha Shel Simcha

Best for: Peak reception dancing

“Mahapecha Shel Simcha” by Omer Adam and Lior Narkis brings modern Israeli party energy to the reception. It is a strong late-evening choice when the formal moments are complete and the goal is simple: keep the room moving.

A song like this gives younger guests something current while keeping the playlist rooted in Israeli music. Ask your DJ to mix it into a broader dance set rather than treating it like a ceremonial selection.

How to Build the Right Jewish Wedding Playlist

A list of popular songs is useful, but timing and context make the music work. Before finalizing the playlist, talk through these questions with your band, DJ, officiant, and venue:

  • Which moments need their own cue? Note the processional, chuppah entrances, glass breaking, recessional, reception entrance, first dance, parent dances, hora, and final song.
  • Which exact versions do you want? Traditional titles often have several melodies, tempos, and recordings. Share links rather than titles alone.
  • How long should the hora last? Plan enough time for both families and friends to join without exhausting the couple before the rest of the reception.
  • What fits your community? Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Mizrahi, Israeli, Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, interfaith, and multicultural weddings may use different songs and customs.
  • Are there vocalist considerations? If Kol Isha is relevant for your guests, confirm vocalist choices with your rabbi and musicians early.
  • Where can your story enter? A familiar playlist can carry the traditions, while a custom Jewish wedding song can make the first dance, parent tribute, or surprise gift entirely your own.

Traditional Songs, Personal Story

The most memorable wedding music balances communal joy with personal meaning. “Siman Tov u’Mazel Tov” can bring every generation into one voice. “Hava Nagila” can pull the room into a circle. “Dodi Li” can quiet everyone beneath the chuppah. Then one song written around how you met, the families joining together, and the future you are building can belong only to you.
